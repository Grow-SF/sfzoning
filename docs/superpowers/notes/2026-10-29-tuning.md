## Diagnosis (1948/1 in detail, checked on the other sheets)

Images are in the session scratch dir
`/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-sbuss-workspace-sfzoning/22a19e20-c93d-417a-b9b0-6c9e893395dd/scratchpad/diag/`.

1. **No systematic offset.** Mean residual vector (-0.03, 0.01) ft; radial component
   (scan corner vs modern block centroid) median -0.25 ft. Measured along 1447 modern block
   edges, the drawn stroke centre sits a median 1.8 ft inside the DataSF lot line (outer
   stroke edge +1.6 ft), and our extracted edge sits on the stroke centre (median -0.1 ft).
   Growing/shrinking every extracted block by +-1..2 px made the score worse in both
   directions (buffer 0: f6 0.598; -1 px 0.494; +1 px 0.574; +1.5 px 0.500). The
   stroke-vs-lot-line convention is not the cause. (`resid_1948_1.png`, `ov_1948_1_1900_3300.png`)
2. **Residuals are per-corner, not regional.** The mean residual of neighbours within
   150/300/600/1200 ft explains 1/3/5/0% of residual power; removing a per-block translation
   leaves rms 5.4 ft vs 5.7 ft expected for pure noise. Residual covariance is isotropic
   (21.7, 21.9 ft^2). Facing edges across a street are anti-correlated (r = -0.4 in the
   outward-offset sign), i.e. a street's two edges are displaced *together* by a few ft:
   the draftsman placed that street a few feet off. Per-edge scatter of the drawn stroke
   against the modern line: IQR -5.0..+1.3 ft, within-edge straightness 1.0 ft. At
   1:4800-ish drafting, 0.01 in of pen error is 4 ft. This is drafting precision, at a finer
   scale than any smooth warp should follow.
3. **Smoothing was too stiff - but only moderately.** 5-fold cross-validation on the final
   inliers, holding out whole 300 ft tiles (so the warp must predict blocks it was not fit
   on), improves from 1.0 down to 0.01-0.1 on every sheet tested, then gets worse (fits
   noise). CV share within 6 ft, smoothing 1.0 -> 0.1 (-> best):
   1948/1 0.566 -> 0.613 (0.631 @0.01); 2-and-3 0.660 -> 0.744 (0.779 @0.003-0.01);
   3 0.862 -> 0.924 (0.934); 4 0.930 -> 0.951 (0.951 @0.1); 5a 0.581 -> 0.606 (0.606 @0.1);
   6a 0.458 -> 0.503 (0.503 @0.1); 1960/1 0.532 -> 0.612 (0.633 @0.003); 1960/7 0.632 ->
   0.669 (0.701 @0.01); 1960/10 0.524 -> 0.608 (0.624 @0.01); 1960/12 0.391 -> 0.424.
   With 800 ft tiles 1948/1 peaks at 0.03 (0.605) vs 0.543 at 1.0. The in-sample score is
   always higher than CV (1948/1 at 0.03: in-sample 0.74, held-out 0.63): **the sheet
   score is in-sample and gets more optimistic as smoothing drops.**
4. **Leverage (staying true to history).** On real inlier density (1948/1, 1960/1): a lone
   GCP moved 40 ft pulls the fit 1.0 / 2.8 / 4.8 / 7.3 ft at smoothing 1 / 0.1 / 0.03 /
   0.01; every corner within 250 ft (~one block, 6-8 corners) moved 15 ft pulls it
   2.2 / 5.5 / 8.1 / 10.8 ft. At 0.03 a single block that really moved would be half
   absorbed before classify_changes sees it; 0.1 keeps it at about a third.
5. **Corner-detection errors in the tail (fixed).** A gallery of >= 9 ft residuals
   (`gallery_1948_1_9.png` was rendered before the fix and then overwritten by the after-fix gallery; `gallery_1948_1_5.png` shows the 5-8 ft band) showed about half were extraction errors: a
   solid block's polygon grew 5-7 px into the street. Cause (`terr_3230_2847.png`): on photos
   the long-run street seeds stop short of the kerb where specks or a ragged edge break the
   runs; the white margin between seeds and kerb becomes its own thin "candidate", which
   joins an alley mouth, touches street along one long side only (one contact), and is
   judged "street beyond it -> block material". After the fix the >= 9 ft gallery is mostly
   genuine drawing-vs-modern differences.
6. **Rough fit must stay stiff (found while tuning).** `anchors.rough_fit` used
   config.TPS_SMOOTHING, and refine's coarse stages were TPS_SMOOTHING x 1000/100/10.
   Lowering TPS_SMOOTHING to 0.03 therefore made the rough fit a near-interpolation of
   coarse street-name anchors and the first refine stage 33x looser: 1960/10 went from key
   distance 4.7 ft to 34 ft (mislock, 225 "real change"). Decoupled (below).
7. **Gross misplacements come from the rough fit, not from tuning.** 1948/10 (key 22,000 ft),
   1960/3, 4, 5 (180-5,900 ft), 1960/2 and 9 (37-110 ft) are placed wrong by the label
   anchors (1960 has few street-pair anchors: sheet 4 has 6). The score flags them
   (not_converged / ambiguous_placement / below_threshold with very few scored corners), and

   distance: n_scored 332 of ~1100 corners is the only in-score hint.
8. **Real change.** On 1948/1 the Embarcadero waterfront and Golden Gateway area come out

   waterfront corners are at f6 0.62. On 1948/2-and-3 a north-south run of real change in
   the Western Addition plus no_equivalent hulls; the Van Ness corridor is

## Assessment against the owner's bar

- Clean, unchanged grids at good drafting quality reach the bar: the Richmond on 1948/3 and
  /4 is 95-96% within 6 ft. Whole sheets do not: 1948 best 0.92, typical 0.6-0.8; 1960
  best placed sheets 0.69-0.75.
- The remaining error is per-corner (sigma ~4 ft per axis on 1948/1), isotropic, not
  regionally coherent, and comes from where the draftsman put each street (2-3 px at
  2.1-2.9 ft/px), not from paper curvature, stroke convention, or a too-stiff warp. A smooth
  warp cannot remove it without snapping edges, which the owner ruled out. Even fitting
  noise in-sample (smoothing 0.01) leaves most sheets below 95% at 8 ft.
- So with these sources, "95% of corners within 6-8 ft" sends every sheet to review.
  Options for the owner: (a) keep the bar and treat the review page as the normal path;
  (b) define the bar as a median/p80 (1948 median p50 ~4.5 ft, 1960 ~4.8 ft); (c) keep
  95% but at ~12 ft (p95 is 8-13 ft on well-placed sheets), i.e. "within two pen widths";
  (d) rely on step two's snapping of zoning boundaries for the 10/10 target and accept
  ~4 ft median for block outlines.
- Separate, real failures: 1948/10 and 1960/2, 3, 4, 5, 9 are misplaced by the rough
  (label-anchor) fit. They fail loudly, but they need either better 1960 anchors or the
  answer-key fallback (currently only used when the rough fit returns nothing).
